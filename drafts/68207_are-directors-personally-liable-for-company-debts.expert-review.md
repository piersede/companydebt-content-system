## Claims that need your verification / sign-off (YMYL, compliance-sensitive)

1. **Disqualification timing (TOP PRIORITY - this was the corrected legal fix).**
   Stated in three places (at-a-glance box, What Remains Personal list, FAQ) as:
   "Disqualification proceedings are separate from debt liability and run on their own statutory
   timetable. For an insolvent company, the Secretary of State or Official Receiver generally has
   three years from the date of insolvency, or dissolution, to issue proceedings under the Company
   Directors Disqualification Act 1986."
   BEFORE (incorrect, now removed): "Disqualification can be sought for up to two years after the
   conduct report" / "within two years of the conduct report under section 7".
   Basis: s.7(2) CDDA 1986 as amended by the Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act 2015
   extended the time limit from 2 years to 3 years from the date the company became insolvent.
   CONFIRM the three-year framing and that "insolvency, or dissolution" is the right trigger wording.

2. **HMRC four-way split** (HMRC PLN/JSLN section):
   - Ordinary VAT/PAYE/Corporation Tax normally remain a company debt in liquidation; not every
     unpaid VAT/PAYE bill creates personal liability.
   - NIC Personal Liability Notice: s.121C Social Security Administration Act 1992, fraud or neglect
     of a culpable officer (HMRC NIM12209).
   - Joint and Several Liability Notice: Finance Act 2020, extends to directors, shadow directors and
     connected persons in tax-avoidance, tax-evasion and repeated-insolvency cases (GOV.UK JSLN overview).
   - VAT fraudulent evasion penalty transferable to a dishonest director/managing officer (HMRC DMBM530170).
   CONFIRM each route is stated at the right strength and that the "not automatic" framing is correct.

3. **BHS wrongful trading example** (boxed, wrongful trading section):
   "In Wright v Chappell (Re BHS Group Ltd), the High Court found the knowledge condition for
   wrongful trading was met from 8 September 2015 and ordered two directors to each contribute
   GBP6.5m to the companies' assets."
   Source: https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Wright-v-Chappell-Ors.pdf
   CONFIRM the date, the "each contribute GBP6.5m" figure, and case citation are stated accurately.

4. **Phoenix / prohibited-name section** (ss.216-217 IA 1986):
   Five-year restriction on a prohibited name after insolvent liquidation; s.217 personal liability
   for the new company's debts; also a criminal offence; statutory exceptions / court permission exist.
   CONFIRM the five-year period, the s.217 personal-liability consequence, and that the de-escalation
   line ("not simply starting another business") does not understate the risk.

5. **Creditor duty** (misfeasance section): "Once insolvency is actual or likely, under section 172(3)
   of the Companies Act 2006, the duty to promote the company's success becomes subject to the
   requirement to have regard to the interests of creditors." CONFIRM phrasing post-BTI v Sequana.

6. **Personal guarantees / government lending:** "a Bounce Back Loan carries no personal guarantee,
   but larger CBILS facilities sometimes do." CONFIRM (BBLS 100% government-backed, no PG; CBILS PG
   rules for facilities above GBP250,000, no PPR).

7. **Core statutory routes (unchanged but load-bearing):** wrongful trading s.214 (incl. s.214(7)
   shadow-director extension in the new FAQ), fraudulent trading s.213 IA 1986 / s.993 CA 2006 with
   up to 10 years' imprisonment, misfeasance s.212, preference s.239, HMRC PLN bankruptcy-petition
   threshold GBP5,000. CONFIRM all statute numbers and figures.
