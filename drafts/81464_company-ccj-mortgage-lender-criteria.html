Your company has a County Court Judgment (CCJ) against it. Your own credit file is clean. You are about to apply for a mortgage, and you cannot find a straight answer to the only question that matters: do you have to tell them?

We could not find a straight answer either, so we went and read the criteria.

This page records what 14 UK mortgage lenders publish about judgments registered against a limited company, checked directly against their intermediary criteria on 12 August 2026. Each row carries its own source and its own check date, because this is the kind of information that goes stale quietly.

The Short Answer on Company CCJs and Mortgages

Three of the 14 ask. Eleven publish nothing at all about company judgments.

That ratio is the finding, and it cuts both ways. Most lenders write their credit rules around your personal credit file, where a company CCJ never appears. A minority ask a question that reaches past it.

So the honest answer to "do I have to declare it" is that it depends entirely on which lender you approach, and you can establish that before you apply rather than after.

Which Lenders Ask About a Company CCJ

The three that ask belong to two banking groups. That is worth sitting with for a moment, because it means the market is not really split 3 to 11. It is split 2 to 12, by group.

Halifax and BM Solutions are both Lloyds Banking Group, and they use near-identical wording. The credit-history question must be answered yes where a CCJ or default has been registered against a self-employed applicant's business in the last six years. No minimum value. No shareholding floor.

Barclays takes a different route to a harder place. Its criteria name a limited company in which the applicant holds more than a 15% shareholding, carrying outstanding judgments totalling more than £5,000, and put it in the list of things that must be declined. Documentary evidence that the dispute has been settled is the only way back.

Two approaches, two very different consequences. One is a question you answer honestly and then discuss. The other is a decline you have to argue your way out of with paperwork.

Where the Company CCJ Thresholds Cluster

Read down the thresholds column and a number keeps reappearing. £500.

HSBC, Virgin Money, Skipton, Coventry, Leeds and The Mortgage Works all set their limit at £500 or below, on your personal judgments. Look-back windows cluster just as tightly, at three years and six years.

Against that backdrop, the one company-judgment value threshold in the whole sample stands out. Barclays sets it at £5,000, ten times the common personal figure.

We read that gap as a rough proxy for how the two things are viewed. A £600 personal judgment marks you as someone who did not pay. A £600 judgment against a trading company is closer to background noise, and lenders who bother to look at company judgments at all are looking for something more substantial.

Residential Versus Buy-to-Let Is Not the Clean Split It Looks

There is a widespread assumption that company-judgment questions live in buy-to-let lending, where the company is part of the transaction, and not in ordinary residential lending. It is a reasonable assumption. It is also wrong.

Barclays applies the same 15% and £5,000 wording in its residential criteria and its buy-to-let criteria. Lloyds Banking Group asks the business question in residential lending through Halifax and in buy-to-let lending through BM Solutions, including its limited company range.

We got this wrong ourselves, and it is worth saying so. An earlier version of our main CCJ guide described the Barclays rule as buy-to-let only. It is not, and we corrected it the day we checked.

Satisfied Judgments and What Lenders Do With Them

If your company's judgment is still outstanding, satisfying it changes your position with some lenders and not others.

With Barclays it matters a great deal. The criterion is keyed to outstanding judgments, and evidence of a settled dispute is the stated route to consideration. Paying the judgment is not a formality there, it is the difference between a decline and a conversation.

With the Lloyds Banking Group question it changes nothing about the disclosure. The six-year question does not distinguish satisfied from unsatisfied, so a company CCJ you paid off in year two still has to be declared in year five. It may well help once declared. It does not remove the obligation to declare it.

In the cases we handle, this catches people out more than any other detail on this page. Directors clear the judgment, assume the slate is clean, and answer a declaration question wrongly without meaning to.

What a Clean Personal Credit File Does and Does Not Prove

Here is the trap, stated plainly.

Your company's CCJ is not on your personal credit file. That is correct, it is the general rule for a limited company, and we explain the mechanics in our guide to company CCJs and director liability.

Skipton's wording shows why that is not the end of it. Its limit applies to "all CCJs on the credit report", which a company judgment is not. Halifax's question is not tied to your credit report at all. It asks what has been registered against your business.

Those are two different tests. A credit search answers the first. Only you can answer the second, and you answer it on a form, under a declaration.

So the practical position is unglamorous but manageable. A clean file is good news and it is not a defence. If a lender asks about your business and you say no when the answer is yes, the problem you have created is a disclosure problem, and that is a worse one to have than a company CCJ.

What to Do Before You Apply, With a Company CCJ Outstanding

Most people reading this are not worried about the paperwork. They are worried that a bad year in the business is about to cost them the house, and that they will not see it coming until the decline lands. That is a reasonable fear and it is largely avoidable, because the question is knowable in advance.

Find out what the lender asks before you get to the declaration. A broker can pull the criteria for you in minutes, and the question you want answered is specific: does the credit-history question reach business judgments, and if so, over what period.

Check the company's position at Companies House and on the Register of Judgments before anyone else does. Know the amount, the date and whether it is satisfied.

If it is outstanding and you can clear it, clear it. It removes the Barclays trigger entirely and it improves the conversation everywhere else.

And if the judgment is one of several, or the company is struggling to pay it, the mortgage is not the real problem. An unsatisfied judgment is one of the clearest evidential routes to a winding-up petition, and at that point you need to look at the company before you look at the house.

FAQs on Company CCJs and Mortgage Applications

Will a mortgage lender find my company's CCJ if it does not ask?

Not through a standard personal credit search, because the judgment sits against the company and not against you. The Register of Judgments, Orders and Fines is public, so it is findable by anyone who looks, but a routine mortgage credit check on an individual does not look there.

Where a lender assesses your business accounts as part of a self-employed application, the judgment or the dispute behind it can surface in the figures instead.

Does it matter how much of the company I own?

With Barclays, yes. Its criterion applies where you hold more than a 15% shareholding, so a small minority stake falls outside it.

With the Lloyds Banking Group question there is no shareholding floor at all. What matters there is whether you are self-employed and whether the judgment was registered against your business.

I am a sole trader, not a limited company. Is my position the same?

No, and your position is considerably worse. A sole trader is not a separate legal person, so a judgment against the business is a judgment against you. It goes on your personal credit file and every threshold in the table above applies to it directly.

The whole distinction this page turns on only exists because a limited company is legally separate from its directors.

Should I just apply to a lender that does not ask?

Choosing a lender whose criteria you meet is ordinary, sensible mortgage broking. Choosing one specifically to avoid a question you would have to answer yes to is a different thing, and a broker is the right person to steer that.

Bear in mind that a lender's criteria today are not its criteria in six months, and that the eleven lenders publishing nothing have not promised never to ask. Silence in published criteria is not a guarantee.

The judgment against my company is wrong. What can I do before I apply?

A judgment entered in error or without the company having a chance to defend can sometimes be set aside, which is a matter for a litigation solicitor rather than for us. If it is set aside, it comes off the register.

Paying it in full within one calendar month of judgment also removes it from the register entirely. After that window, paying it marks it satisfied rather than removing it, and it stays visible for six years.

Methodology and Disclosure

We read each lender's published intermediary lending criteria directly, in a browser, on 12 August 2026. Lender criteria sites block automated retrieval, so this was collected by hand rather than scraped, and every entry in the table is drawn from the lender's own published wording rather than from a broker summary or a comparison site.

Where a lender publishes separate residential and buy-to-let criteria, the book we checked is named in the table. Barclays and BM Solutions were checked in more than one book, because the company-judgment wording appears in more than one.

"No published criterion" means exactly that. It means we found nothing in the lender's published criteria addressing judgments against a company, and it is not a statement that the lender will never ask. Underwriting discretion sits behind every published rule, and criteria not published to intermediaries are not visible to us.

Two lenders we intended to include, TSB and Aldermore, could not be reached from our research environment and are absent rather than silent. We would rather show you a gap than guess at a row.

The Precise criteria guide available to us carries its own date of 22 May 2023. We have kept the row and flagged it, because presenting a three-year-old document as a current check would be the exact failure this page is trying to avoid.
