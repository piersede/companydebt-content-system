Mortgage lenders publish their credit rules for brokers, not for borrowers. They are public, but only if you know they exist and which of a lender's several rule books to open. That is the main reason this question is so hard to answer by searching, and it is why we went and read the criteria ourselves.

A County Court Judgment (CCJ) against your limited company does not land on your personal credit file. Most directors work that out fairly quickly. The harder question is whether a lender asks you about it anyway.

We read the published intermediary criteria of 14 UK lenders on 12 August 2026 and recorded what each one asks. Every row below carries its source and the date we read it.

The Short Answer on Company CCJs and Mortgages

Three of the 14 publish an explicit company-CCJ criterion. At the other eleven we found no published company-CCJ criterion at all.

Those are different claims and the difference is worth your attention. Three lenders have written the question down, so the position is knowable before an application goes in. Eleven have written nothing either way, which tells you about their published criteria and nothing about what an underwriter will ask when they open your file.

Most readers want silence to mean safety. It does not. It means there is no rule to rely on and none to fall foul of, and nothing stops a question arriving late, when you have already paid for a valuation.

Our advice is therefore split. With Halifax, BM Solutions and Barclays you can plan around a known rule. With the rest, ask your broker to put the question to the lender rather than assuming the answer.

Which Lenders Ask About a Company CCJ

Two of the three sit inside the same banking group, so you are looking at two independent positions rather than three.

Halifax and BM Solutions are both Lloyds Banking Group and use near-identical wording. If you are self-employed and a CCJ or default has been registered against your business in the last six years, the credit-history question must be answered yes. There is no minimum value, so a £300 judgment counts. There is no shareholding floor, so a 5% stake counts.

In practice this is the mild version. You declare it, it feeds the credit score, and an underwriter asks for the background. Plenty of directors get mortgages this way. What damages the application is not the judgment but a wrong answer to the question.

Barclays is the one that should change your plans. Where you hold more than 15% of a limited company and that company carries outstanding judgments totalling more than £5,000, Barclays lists it among the things that must be declined. It sits in the decline list, not the refer list.

The single exception is documentary evidence that the dispute has been settled. So if that describes your company and Barclays is on your shortlist, clear the judgment and get the paperwork before you apply. Of everything on this page, that is the fact most likely to change what you do this month.

How the 14 Lenders Compare on Company CCJs

Where a lender publishes different criteria for residential and buy-to-let lending, the book we checked is stated.

Where the Company CCJ Thresholds Cluster

Six of the 14 draw their line for your personal judgments at £500 or below: HSBC, Virgin Money, Skipton, Coventry, Leeds and The Mortgage Works. Barclays is tighter still at £200. Look-back windows bunch at three and six years.

Those limits are lower than most people expect, and they have a blunt consequence. One unpaid £600 invoice that reached judgment can put you outside six of these lenders on its own, whatever your income. Personal judgments are being used as a character test, so the size of the debt barely enters into it.

Now compare the only company-judgment figure anyone publishes. Barclays sets that at £5,000, twenty-five times its own personal limit.

We think that gap is the most informative thing in the dataset. Lenders evidently accept that a trading company collects the odd dispute, and that a small judgment against one proves very little. The rule is aimed somewhere specific: a company that has been sued for real money and has not paid.

It is also a useful corrective if the eleven silent lenders have reassured you. The one firm that has examined this in public did not decide company judgments were irrelevant. It decided they became relevant above £5,000.

Residential Versus Buy-to-Let Is Not the Clean Split It Looks

Ask around and you will be told that company-judgment questions belong to buy-to-let lending, where the company is part of the deal, and that ordinary residential lending leaves your business alone. We checked that twice, because we did not believe the answer the first time.

Barclays carries the 15% and £5,000 criterion in near-identical wording in both books. In the residential criteria it sits under the heading "Adverse credit history, Residential", inside the list of things that must be declined, with no buy-to-let wording anywhere near it.

Lloyds Banking Group reaches the same place by a different route, asking the business question in residential lending through Halifax and in buy-to-let lending through BM Solutions, its limited company range included.

If you are buying a home to live in and assumed none of this touched you, drop that assumption now.

We had it wrong ourselves until we looked. An earlier version of our main CCJ guide called the Barclays rule buy-to-let only. It is not, and we corrected the page the day we checked.

Satisfied Judgments and What Lenders Do With Them

Paying the judgment off moves the dial a long way with one lender and not at all with another, which is worth knowing before the money goes out.

With Barclays it is decisive. The criterion is keyed to outstanding judgments, and evidence of a settled dispute is the stated route back into consideration. Clearing the debt there is the difference between a decline and an application.

With the Lloyds group question it changes nothing about the disclosure. The six-year question makes no distinction between satisfied and unsatisfied, so a company CCJ you paid in year two must still be declared in year five. Paying it will help once you have declared it. It does not remove the obligation.

By the time a director calls us, this is the detail they have most often got wrong. They clear the judgment, take that as the end of the matter, and answer the declaration honestly but incorrectly.

What a Clean Personal Credit File Does and Does Not Prove

The whole question turns on a mismatch between two systems, and once you see it the rest follows.

Credit reference data is organised around people. Your company is a separate legal person, so its judgment is filed against the company and never reaches your file. A lender searching your record therefore finds nothing, correctly.

The application form is organised around something else entirely: what you know. Skipton's limit applies to "all CCJs on the credit report", which a company judgment is not. Halifax does not mention your credit report at all. It asks what has been registered against your business.

So the lender runs one test and asks a second, and a company CCJ sits in the space between them. The search cannot find it. Only you can answer it, in a box, under a declaration you sign.

That is where the real risk lies, and it is not the risk most people are worried about. A company CCJ is a fact you can explain. A mortgage obtained on an answer that was wrong is a problem that outlives the judgment by years, and we would not advise anyone to take that trade.

What to Do Before You Apply, With a Company CCJ Outstanding

Most directors reading this are not worried about a form. They are worried that a bad year in the business is about to cost them the house, and that nobody will tell them until the decline arrives. That fear is reasonable, and it is mostly avoidable, because every one of these rules can be checked in advance.

Ask your broker to pull the lender's criteria before you get anywhere near a declaration. The question is narrow enough to answer in one call: does the credit-history question reach business judgments, and over what period.

Look up the company's own position at Companies House and on the Register of Judgments before a lender does. Know the amount, the date, and whether it is marked satisfied.

If it is outstanding and you can afford to clear it, clear it. That removes the Barclays trigger completely and improves every conversation that follows.

And if this judgment is one of several, or the company genuinely cannot pay it, the mortgage is the smaller problem. An unsatisfied judgment is one of the strongest pieces of evidence a creditor can take to a winding-up petition. Sort the company out first. The house depends on it more than it depends on the application.

FAQs on Company CCJs and Mortgage Applications

Will a mortgage lender find my company's CCJ if it does not ask?

Not through a standard personal credit search, because the judgment sits against the company and not against you. The Register of Judgments, Orders and Fines is public, so it is findable by anyone who looks, but a routine mortgage credit check on an individual does not look there.

Where a lender assesses business accounts as part of a self-employed application, the judgment or the dispute behind it can surface in the figures instead.

Discovery is the wrong thing to plan around in any case. The exposure that matters is the declaration, because an inaccurate answer is a problem long after a judgment has dropped off the register.

Does it matter how much of the company I own?

With Barclays, yes. Its criterion applies where you hold more than a 15% shareholding, so a small minority stake falls outside it.

With the Lloyds Banking Group question there is no shareholding floor at all. What matters there is self-employment and whether the judgment was registered against the business.

So a 10% stake sits outside the Barclays criterion and squarely inside the Lloyds one. Shareholding is not a general defence, it is a Barclays-specific threshold.

I am a sole trader, not a limited company. Is my position the same?

No, and your position is considerably worse. A sole trader is not a separate legal person, so a judgment against the business is a judgment against you. It goes on your personal credit file and every threshold in the table above applies to it directly.

The whole distinction this page turns on only exists because a limited company is legally separate from its directors.

Should I just apply to one of the eleven with no published rule?

Choosing a lender whose criteria you meet is ordinary, sensible mortgage broking. Choosing one specifically to dodge a question you would have to answer yes to is a different thing, and a broker is the right person to steer that.

The bigger flaw in the plan is that you would be relying on an absence. Those eleven lenders have not said they will not ask. They have simply not published a rule either way, and nothing stops an underwriter raising it on a self-employed case. Criteria also change, as this page's monthly re-check exists to catch.

The judgment against my company is wrong. What can I do before I apply?

A judgment entered in error or without the company having a chance to defend can sometimes be set aside, which is a matter for a litigation solicitor rather than for us. If it is set aside, it comes off the register.

Paying it in full within one calendar month of judgment also removes it from the register entirely. Miss that window and paying only marks it satisfied, and it stays visible for six years.

That one-month deadline is the most valuable thing on this page for anyone with a fresh judgment, and it is the deadline most often missed. If the debt is genuinely owed and affordable, pay it inside the month.

We read each lender's published intermediary lending criteria directly, in a browser, on 12 August 2026. Lender criteria sites block automated retrieval, so this was collected by hand rather than scraped, and every entry in the table is drawn from the lender's own published wording rather than from a broker summary or a comparison site.
