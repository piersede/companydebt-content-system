Quick Answer: What Happens if You Cannot Pay VAT

Call HMRC's Business Payment Support Service on 0300 200 3835 before the payment deadline if possible, or as soon as you realise you cannot pay. Ask for a Time to Pay (TTP) arrangement.

HMRC will assess whether your business is viable and whether you can sustain a repayment schedule. If they agree, you get an instalment plan (typically 6 to 12 months), enforcement is suspended, and surcharges may be frozen. If you do not call, HMRC will escalate through surcharges, penalties, debt collection, statutory demands, and ultimately a winding-up petition.

We tell every director: the phone call costs nothing. The silence costs everything.

HMRC Time to Pay: How to Negotiate a VAT Payment Plan

Time to Pay is HMRC's formal instalment arrangement. It is the single most effective tool for dealing with VAT debt, and we recommend it to every director before any other option.

How to apply. Call the Business Payment Support Service on 0300 200 3835. You do not need a solicitor or an insolvency practitioner to make this call. Explain that you cannot pay the VAT, explain why (cash-flow timing, a bad debt, a delayed contract), and propose a repayment schedule you can realistically meet.

What HMRC assesses. Is the business viable? Can you demonstrate that the repayment schedule is sustainable? Are you current on all other tax obligations?

HMRC is significantly more likely to agree if you are up to date on PAYE, Corporation Tax, and current VAT returns. If you are behind on everything, TTP becomes harder to negotiate.

Typical terms. 6 to 12 months, with equal monthly instalments. Interest accrues on the outstanding balance at the HMRC late payment rate (currently 7.5%). You must keep up with current VAT obligations as they fall due during the TTP period.

Miss a payment on the TTP or fall behind on a current return, and HMRC can terminate the arrangement and resume enforcement immediately.

We find that directors who call HMRC proactively, before enforcement starts, have an acceptance rate we estimate at 70 to 80%. Directors who call after receiving a statutory demand see that rate drop to under 30%. Timing is the single biggest factor.

What Happens to VAT Debt in Insolvency: Preferential Status

Since December 2020, VAT is a secondary preferential debt in insolvency. If your company enters liquidation, HMRC's VAT claim ranks ahead of floating charge holders and unsecured creditors in the creditor priority order.

This changed the dynamics significantly. HMRC recovers more from insolvency than before 2020, which makes them less willing to negotiate and more willing to petition, because the outcome of liquidation is now better for them.

We stress this because directors who remember the old regime (where HMRC was an unsecured creditor for VAT) assume HMRC will be patient. They are less patient than they were, because the incentive structure has changed. A winding-up petition now recovers more for HMRC than it used to.

Personal Liability for Unpaid VAT

VAT is a tax you collect on behalf of HMRC from your customers. If the company collected VAT and failed to remit it, HMRC can pursue you personally in certain circumstances. The main routes:

  • Wrongful trading. If you continued trading while insolvent and the VAT debt increased during that period, the liquidator can seek a personal contribution under section 214.
  • Joint and several liability. In VAT group registrations, HMRC can pursue any member of the VAT group for the full amount, including the representative member personally in some structures.
  • Fraudulent evasion. Deliberately under-declaring VAT or failing to register when required is a criminal offence carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment.

We are direct: for most directors of legitimate businesses, the personal liability risk from unpaid VAT comes through wrongful trading, not through personal VAT liability notices. The risk is that you continued trading while the company was insolvent and the VAT debt grew. That growth is what the liquidator measures.

What to Do When You Cannot Pay VAT Right Now

  1. Call HMRC today. 0300 200 3835. Ask for Time to Pay. Do not wait for the next letter.
  2. File your VAT return on time even if you cannot pay. Late filing triggers separate penalties. Filing on time and paying late is less damaging than doing neither.
  3. Check whether you are current on other taxes. HMRC assesses TTP applications against your full compliance record. Bring PAYE and Corporation Tax up to date if possible.
  4. If the debt is too large for TTP, speak to a licensed insolvency practitioner. Company Debt connects directors with IPs who can assess whether the business is viable or whether a formal rescue or closure route is appropriate.
