## 1. Executive Decision

The target page already competes strongly and is supported by one of the deeper company-closure and liquidation clusters in the result set. The citation audit confirms that its main weakness is **factual control and legal precision**, not lack of breadth.

The page should become the definitive UK route selector for directors, built around one first question:

> **Can the company pay all its debts, including interest, in full and on time?**

For MVL eligibility, separately explain the statutory declaration that the company will be able to pay its debts, including interest, within 12 months.

The route logic should then distinguish:

- voluntary strike-off for a clean, solvent and inactive company whose affairs are fully settled;
- an MVL for a solvent company where formal liquidation and capital distributions may be worthwhile;
- a CVL where the company cannot pay creditors and rescue is not realistic;
- compulsory liquidation as a creditor- and court-led outcome rather than a route normally selected by directors;
- dormancy where the company may be used again;
- rescue or restructuring where the underlying business remains viable.

### Critical factual corrections

1. **Business Asset Disposal Relief is 18%, not 10%, for qualifying disposals from 6 April 2026.** Qualification is conditional and the £1 million lifetime limit is cumulative.
2. **Bounce Back Loan Scheme facilities did not permit personal guarantees.** Separate personal exposure may arise through fraud, misuse, misrepresentation or other misconduct, but not from a scheme personal guarantee.
3. **An MVL does not write off company debts.** It is a solvent liquidation in which creditors should be paid in full, normally with statutory interest where due, before the surplus is distributed.
4. **Director liability must not be confused with shareholder liability.** Liability limited to unpaid share capital describes the normal shareholder position, not the full legal position of a director.
5. **A personal guarantee is normally the guarantor’s contingent personal liability.** The underlying borrowing remains a company liability.
6. **A director’s loan account can be an asset or a liability of the company.** An overdrawn account is normally an asset recoverable from the director; an account in credit is normally a company liability.
7. **An inaccurate declaration of solvency does not automatically make directors personally liable.** Knowingly making a false declaration can be a criminal offence, while contribution or compensation depends on the facts and any separate claim.
8. **Restoration does not automatically transfer company debts to directors.** It revives the company and its liabilities; personal liability requires a separate legal basis.
9. **Wrongful trading is not established merely because the company traded while insolvent.** The test concerns the point at which there was no reasonable prospect of avoiding insolvent liquidation or administration and whether every step was taken to minimise creditor loss.
10. **A dormant company normally stops filing Company Tax Returns after HMRC accepts dormancy**, unless HMRC asks for one or the company begins trading again. Companies House accounts and confirmation statements still remain due.
11. **The £25,000 strike-off distribution threshold is a tax rule, not a universal MVL break-even point.** The answer depends on shareholder tax circumstances, liquidation costs, remaining BADR allowance and anti-avoidance rules.
12. **The page must not present compulsory liquidation as one of four equal director-controlled routes.**
13. **The exact strike-off process must include the current £13 online and £18 paper fees, majority-director approval, the statutory three-month restrictions and seven-day notification requirement.**
14. **The unsupported claim that a company bank account is commonly frozen within 24 hours of petition advertisement must be removed.**
15. **The claim that compulsory liquidation costs the company £10,000+ must be removed or replaced with an itemised, sourced explanation of petition, deposit, estate and office-holder costs.**

### Structural decision

Use this framing:

> **Three director-controlled closure routes, plus compulsory liquidation if a creditor or the court acts first.**

Then add dormancy and rescue as alternatives to closure.

### Overall direction

- Keep the URL and its ownership of the broad query.
- Correct the factual record across the full closure and liquidation cluster before changing presentation.
- Rebuild the opening 30–40% around a solvency-led decision tree and a route table.
- Add one complete pre-closure checklist for solvent strike-off users.
- Distinguish statutory strike-off eligibility from practical suitability.
- Shorten route descriptions and link to the existing specialist pages.
- Use the June 2026 Insolvency Service MVL study as first-class information gain.
- Replace automatic tax conclusions with a transparent MVL-versus-strike-off calculation.
- Add a liability matrix covering ordinary debts, HMRC, guarantees, Bounce Back Loans, director loans and conduct claims.
- Centralise fees, tax rates, deadlines, eligibility and route facts in one structured source of truth.
- Preserve the calm, direct tone and licensed-practitioner authority. A clean solvent company should be allowed to reach DS01 guidance without unnecessary fear.

## 3. Search Intent and Ideal Page Type

### Practical problem behind the query

The user is usually not looking for a single administrative instruction. They are trying to identify which legal route fits their company before they make an expensive or irreversible mistake.

They may need to establish:

- whether the company is solvent;
- whether it owes HMRC, suppliers, lenders or employees;
- whether the company has retained profits or other assets;
- whether strike-off is permitted and safe;
- whether an MVL is tax-efficient after fees and personal tax circumstances;
- whether insolvency advice is urgent;
- what happens to personal guarantees, director loans, staff and company assets;
- whether the company could instead be rescued or left dormant.

### Dominant intent

**Mixed informational and commercial decision support.**

The page should help the director select a route and understand the next action. It should not behave as a generic definition page or assume every visitor requires liquidation.

### Secondary intents

- step-by-step strike-off process;
- MVL versus dissolution;
- closing with debt;
- closing with no money;
- costs and timescales;
- tax on distributions;
- personal liability;
- employee claims;
- HMRC arrears;
- compulsory liquidation and winding-up petitions;
- dormancy and future reuse.

### Likely user state

The user may be calm and planning a solvent retirement, or under acute creditor pressure. The opening must allow both users to identify themselves immediately.

### Likely fears

- choosing strike-off when creditors can object;
- losing money through unnecessary liquidation fees;
- paying more tax than necessary;
- becoming personally liable;
- committing a breach of duty by continuing to trade;
- losing access to the bank account;
- being disqualified;
- closing the company but leaving debts or assets unresolved.

### Ideal user journey

1. **Identify solvency:** Can all debts and interest be paid within 12 months?
2. **Identify the objective:** Permanent closure, temporary pause or rescue.
3. **Compare viable routes:** Eligibility, cost, tax, control and timescale.
4. **Verify material risks:** Creditors, staff, assets, guarantees and HMRC.
5. **Take the next step:** Apply for strike-off, speak to an accountant or obtain insolvency advice.

### Ideal page type

A **hybrid route-selector and expert guide**, supported by the site’s dedicated strike-off, MVL, CVL and compulsory-liquidation pages.

### Strongest structural opportunity

The target should separate **route selection** from **route detail**.

The main page should answer:

- Which route applies?
- Why?

- What is the immediate risk?

Retain only questions that are not already fully answered. The current FAQ repeats route conclusions and contains the unsafe “debts are written off” answer.

## 18. Final SEO Verdict

The leading results compete because they solve a classification problem before explaining a process. GOV.UK supplies the authoritative framework. Begbies Traynor and Real Business Rescue add practitioner guidance, urgency pathways and deep supporting clusters. Formation and accountancy sites satisfy straightforward strike-off intent.

Company Debt is already well placed. It has:

- a strong exact-match page;
- a fast solvency-led opening;
- licensed-practitioner authority;
- extensive route-specific content;
- director-focused risk guidance;
- an existing insolvency tool;
- clear commercial relevance.

The citation audit confirms that the real weakness is **confidence without adequate factual control**. The page contains material errors involving BADR, Bounce Back Loans, MVL debt treatment, director liability, guarantees, director loans, wrongful trading and dormancy. It also omits important operational facts such as the seven-day DS01 notification duty and the risk that remaining assets pass to the Crown.

The correct approach is not a wholesale rewrite or wider cluster expansion. It is to:

1. correct the factual record across the entire closure cluster;
2. make this URL the clear route selector and simple pre-closure checklist;
3. keep route depth on the existing specialist pages;
4. distinguish director choice from creditor/court action;
5. distinguish company debt from separate director exposure;
6. add dormancy and rescue as legitimate alternatives;
7. use official 2026 MVL data as information gain;
8. replace simplistic tax recommendations with a transparent calculation;
9. control every changing fee, rate, deadline and annual limit centrally.

With these changes, the page can be stronger than the current result set: as clear as GOV.UK, more practical than formation-agent guides, more balanced for clean solvent companies and more legally precise than competing insolvency lead-generation pages.

Do not copy competitors’ “four ways”, “any debt means CVL”, “over £25,000 means MVL” or automatic-director-liability formulas. The opportunity is to become the page that tells a director **which routes are actually available, what must be completed before closure, what remains a company liability, what can create personal exposure and which facts have been checked against current official sources**.
