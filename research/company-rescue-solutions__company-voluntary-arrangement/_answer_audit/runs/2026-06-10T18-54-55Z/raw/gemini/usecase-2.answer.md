A Company Voluntary Arrangement (CVA) in the UK involves several costs, primarily related to the fees of the Insolvency Practitioner (IP) who acts as the nominee and then the supervisor of the arrangement. These costs are typically paid from the company's cash flow.

**Relevant Insolvency Route:** Company Voluntary Arrangement (CVA).

**Governing Rule or Official Source:**
The fees, costs, charges, and expenses for a voluntary arrangement are set out in the Insolvency (England and Wales) Rules 2016. The CVA process itself is governed by the Insolvency Act 1986.

**How Much Does a CVA Cost?**
There are no set fees for Insolvency Practitioners in the UK, and the exact cost of a CVA can vary significantly based on factors such as the company's size, complexity, the number of creditors, and the value and complexity of assets.

Generally, CVA costs can be broken down into two main components:

1.  **Nominee's Fees (Initial Fees):** These cover the work involved in preparing the CVA proposal, assessing company finances, consulting with creditors, negotiating terms, and arranging the creditors' meeting.
    *   For simple, small-scale CVAs, initial fees can start around £1,500.
    *   More commonly, initial fees for proposal preparation, creditor consultation, and implementation range from £2,500 to £5,000.
    *   Some sources suggest nominee fees for preparing and implementing a CVA can range from £5,000 to £10,000.
    *   For small to medium-sized businesses, the total lifetime cost of a CVA, including both nominee and supervisor fees, can range from £2,000 to £10,000 depending on complexity, the number of creditors, and total debt.
    *   Overall, the total cost for a CVA, covering the IP's fees from proposal preparation to supervision over its duration, typically ranges from £20,000 to £50,000. Larger companies with more complex situations may incur higher costs.

2.  **Supervisor's Fees (Ongoing Fees):** Once the CVA is approved, the appointed supervisor manages and administers the ongoing arrangement. These fees are usually charged periodically throughout the CVA's duration.
    *   Supervisor fees are commonly charged as 10% to 15% of the funds paid to creditors for managing and administering the ongoing arrangement.
    *   For arrangements spanning several years, total supervisor fees can range from £10,000 to £30,000 or more, depending on the company's size and the complexity of the arrangement. For example, a small business CVA with monthly contributions of £3,000 over 5 years (totaling £180,000) could see supervisor fees between £18,000 and £27,000, paid incrementally.

It's important to note that these figures are guides, and Insolvency Practitioners often offer fixed fee options for straightforward cases or charge on a time-cost basis for more complex situations. Creditors also have a say in agreeing to the terms relating to remuneration.

**Who Pays for It?**
The fees for a Company Voluntary Arrangement are typically paid from the company's cash flow. The company makes regular monthly payments to the Insolvency Practitioner (supervisor), and these payments are used to cover the IP's fees before the remainder is distributed to creditors. Company directors do not usually need to fund these fees personally, as they are paid from the company's contributions to the CVA.

**Timelines:**
Most Company Voluntary Arrangements run for a period of three to five years. The nominee IP must prepare the arrangement details and payment schedule within a month of being appointed.
