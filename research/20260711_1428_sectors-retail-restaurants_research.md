# UK Business Finance Research: Insolvency and Business Rescue in Retail and Hospitality

**Key Points:**
*   **Retail and Hospitality Vulnerability:** The structural decline in the UK retail and hospitality sectors is accelerating, driven by compounded pressures from post-pandemic debt, rising statutory wages, and significant hikes in energy and business rates. The evidence heavily suggests that physical, multi-site operators face the most acute risks. 
*   **Insolvency Dominance:** Creditors' Voluntary Liquidations (CVLs) overwhelmingly dominate the insolvency landscape, representing 76% of all corporate insolvencies in the 12 months to May 2026. This indicates a strong trend of director-led closures rather than business rescues.
*   **Sector-Specific Pressures:** While retail faces severe footfall declines exacerbated by extreme weather and e-commerce shifts, restaurants are grappling with structural energy pricing changes (TNUoS) and disproportionate impacts from National Living Wage increases. 
*   **Rescue Mechanisms:** Pre-pack administrations remain a crucial, though sometimes controversial, tool for preserving brand value and intellectual property (e.g., Next's acquisition of Russell & Bromley), while Company Voluntary Arrangements (CVAs) are being leveraged to shed underperforming leaseholds (e.g., Leon). 

*Note: While forecasting insolvency trends involves inherent uncertainty due to fluctuating macroeconomic variables and government policy interventions, the statutory data up to May 2026 provides a robust, empirical foundation for current analysis.*

#### 4. Early Warning Signs for Retailers
*   **Sales and Inventory:** Falling like-for-like (LFL) sales and the necessity of deep discounting to move stock. A critical red flag is rising unsold inventory, leading to severe cash flow bottlenecks.
*   **Arrears:** Accumulation of HMRC liabilities (VAT/PAYE) and falling behind on quarterly rent and business rates.
*   **Supply Chain:** Supplier credit tightening; single points of failure in logistics (as seen with TOFS's reliance on a single third-party logistics provider during peak seasons) [cite: 11] (https://administrationlist.co.uk/insights/original-factory-administration, accessed 11 July 2026).

#### 5. Insolvency and Rescue Options for Retailers
*   **Pre-Pack Administration:** A highly relevant tool for the retail sector to shed debt while preserving intellectual property and brand value. In the case of Russell & Bromley, the operating company failed, but a pre-pack deal allowed Next plc to acquire the brand, domain names, and three viable stores immediately upon the appointment of administrators [cite: 7, 23] (https://zendesk.next.co.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/43434553562129-Russell-Bromley-Frequently-Asked-Questions, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Company Voluntary Arrangement (CVA):** Traditionally used to restructure leasehold portfolios, secure rent reductions, and shed unprofitable stores while avoiding formal administration. (Note: Modella Capital attempted a CVA for TOFS to reduce rent across 88 stores before ultimately entering administration) [cite: 11] (https://administrationlist.co.uk/insights/original-factory-administration, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Creditors' Voluntary Liquidation (CVL):** The most common route (76% of all insolvencies in May 2026) when a retailer is terminally insolvent and no buyer can be found [cite: 20] (https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/company-insolvencies-may-2026/commentary-company-insolvency-statistics-may-2026, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **HMRC Time to Pay:** A vital early intervention to restructure tax arrears over manageable monthly instalments, preventing winding-up petitions. 
*   **Sector-Specific Complexities:** 
    *   *Gift Cards & Customer Deposits:* In administration, gift cards are routinely voided or frozen. For example, upon TOFS's administration, the online shop was immediately closed to preserve cash, and Russell & Bromley customers were advised to contact administrators for outstanding refunds or faulty items, as gift cards were not honoured by the buyer, Next [cite: 11, 23] (https://zendesk.next.co.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/43434553562129-Russell-Bromley-Frequently-Asked-Questions, accessed 11 July 2026).

#### 1. Structural and Financial Pressures
The UK hospitality and restaurant sector is facing an unprecedented "assault on its margins," driving insolvency rates to decadal highs [cite: 24] (https://www.ukhospitality.org.uk/hospitalitys-2026-energy-shock-its-time-to-build-your-defences/, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Energy Inflation:** Energy costs remained around 70% higher in 2025 than pre-2022 levels [cite: 25] (https://opusllp.com/tough-times-for-hospitality-businesses-but-worse-is-yet-to-come/, accessed 11 July 2026). Crucially, from April 2026, the government radically changed how large businesses pay for the national grid via the Transmission Network Use of System (TNUoS) residual charge. Because this is applied as a flat charge per site per day, multi-site restaurant operators are disproportionately affected. TNUoS revenue collection is projected to nearly double from £3.84bn (2025/26) to £7.52bn (2026/27) [cite: 24] (https://www.ukhospitality.org.uk/hospitalitys-2026-energy-shock-its-time-to-build-your-defences/, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Wage and Employer Cost Increases:** Hospitality is highly exposed due to its labor-intensive model. In April 2026, the National Living Wage rose by 4.1% to £12.71 per hour [cite: 26] (https://qxglobalgroup.com/fa/uk/blog/new-hospitality-reality-busy-venues-lower-margins, accessed 11 July 2026). Furthermore, increased employer National Insurance contributions added an effective £2,500 to the cost of employing a full-time member of staff [cite: 25] (https://opusllp.com/tough-times-for-hospitality-businesses-but-worse-is-yet-to-come/, accessed 11 July 2026). UKHospitality estimated the 2026 wage increases alone represent a £1.4bn additional burden for the sector [cite: 26, 27] (https://qxglobalgroup.com/fa/uk/blog/new-hospitality-reality-busy-venues-lower-margins, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Business Rates:** Despite temporary reliefs, the latest revaluations mean the average restaurant faces a 15% increase in business rates (an extra £1,800), while larger premises (rateable value over £500,000) are subjected to a punishing 50.8p high-value multiplier [cite: 26, 27] (https://qxglobalgroup.com/fa/uk/blog/new-hospitality-reality-busy-venues-lower-margins, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Discretionary Spend & Inflation:** Food prices rocketed by 26% between 2012 and 2023, while consumer confidence remains depressed due to the cost-of-living crisis, solidifying a "stay home" habit [cite: 28] (https://www.antonybatty.com/hospitality-cost-increases-april-2026/, accessed 11 July 2026).

#### 4. Early Warning Signs for Restaurants
*   **Metrics:** Falling covers and a sustained drop in average spend per head. Crucially, food-cost and labor-cost percentages creeping up past sustainable thresholds.
*   **Cash Flow Strain:** Rising HMRC arrears (VAT, PAYE) and inability to meet quarterly rent demands or business rates bills.
*   **Operational:** Suppliers withdrawing credit terms (moving to pro-forma), and severe staff shortages due to inability to match rising wage expectations.

#### 5. Insolvency and Rescue Options for Restaurants
*   **Company Voluntary Arrangement (CVA):** Highly effective for multi-site operators to restructure leasehold liabilities. Leon successfully used a CVA in May 2026 to exit administration, shed unprofitable leases, and restructure its debt while maintaining 43 trading sites [cite: 33, 34] (https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/2026/05/29/quantuma-secures-leon-restructure-saving-530-jobs/, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Administration & Pre-Pack:** Used to protect the brand while selling the viable assets to a new or existing owner (as attempted by TGI Fridays UK and executed by Pizza Hut franchisees) [cite: 31, 37] (https://www.thecaterer.com/news/tgi-fridays-pushes-rescue-plan-decision-back-again, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Creditors' Voluntary Liquidation (CVL):** The terminal option for single-site operators or irreparably damaged groups where rescue finance is unavailable [cite: 39] (https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2026/04/20/hospitality-insolvencies-on-the-rise-as-tax-burden-increases-and-dip-in-consumer-confidence-takes-hold/, accessed 11 July 2026).
*   **Sector-Specific Issues:** 
    *   *Leaseholds & Guarantees:* Single-site independents often face directors having signed personal guarantees on leases, making formal corporate insolvency highly dangerous for personal assets. Access to new rescue funding is currently almost impossible without owners providing these guarantees [cite: 38] (https://www.thecaterer.com/news/restaurant-closures-price-bailey-prediction, accessed 11 July 2026). 
    *   *HMRC Time to Pay:* Increasingly difficult to negotiate as HMRC enforcement activity has stiffened noticeably in 2025/2026 [cite: 39] (https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2026/04/20/hospitality-insolvencies-on-the-rise-as-tax-burden-increases-and-dip-in-consumer-confidence-takes-hold/, accessed 11 July 2026).
