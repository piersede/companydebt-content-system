In the UK, company administration is a formal insolvency procedure governed primarily by the Insolvency Act 1986. Its objective is to rescue the company as a going concern, achieve a better result for creditors than in a winding-up, or realise property to make a distribution to secured or preferential creditors.

**Cost of Company Administration**

The cost of company administration is highly variable, depending on the complexity, size, and nature of the company, as well as the assets and liabilities involved and the duration of the administration.

*   **Initial Costs:** Initial administration costs typically range from £10,000 to £15,000.
*   **Pre-administration Fees:** For smaller administrations, pre-administration fees can be between £5,000 and £10,000, which may be higher in pre-pack administrations due to increased preparatory work.
*   **Total Fees:** For complex cases involving business sales, extensive asset realisations, or lengthy administration periods, total fees can easily exceed £20,000.
*   **Fee Basis:** Insolvency Practitioners (IPs) typically charge in one of three ways:
    *   **Time-cost basis:** This is common in more complex cases. Hourly rates for IPs can range from £100 to £300, while staff charge-out rates vary from £115 to £525 per hour, depending on experience and qualifications. Time is usually charged in 6-minute units.
    *   **Fixed fee arrangements:** These may be used for simpler aspects of the administration.
    *   **Percentage of realisations:** In some cases, fees may be calculated as a percentage of funds recovered from asset sales, typically ranging from 3% to 15% of assets realised, though higher percentages may apply for specialist or challenging recoveries.
*   **Other Costs:** Beyond the Administrator's fees, other professional fees and disbursements include agents' valuation fees and legal fees. Legal fees might increase in contested administrations or if a live business is sold, requiring a sale and purchase agreement.

**Governing Rules and Transparency:**
The fee structures for insolvency practitioners must comply with the **Statement of Insolvency Practice 9 (SIP 9)**, which governs the remuneration of insolvency practitioners to ensure transparency and fairness in fee arrangements. SIP 9 mandates that all payments from an estate should be fair, reasonable, and proportionate to the insolvency appointment. Insolvency practitioners are required to provide detailed fee estimates and obtain appropriate approvals before commencing work. Creditors have a right to request further information about fees and expenses and can challenge them if they believe they are excessive.

**Who Pays for Company Administration**

The administrator's fees and other costs of the administration are primarily paid out of the company's assets. These costs are paid before any distributions are made to creditors, including floating charge holders and unsecured creditors. This is why creditors have a direct interest in the level of these costs.

In circumstances where the company lacks sufficient assets to cover the insolvency costs, directors may, in some cases, choose to personally fund basic insolvency procedures. This is done to ensure proper company closure and to avoid potential issues such as director disqualification or personal liability under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986. Additionally, one or more creditors might provide funding to the administrator to facilitate the appointment, particularly before assets are realised. Any pre-administration costs, if not fixed with the directors beforehand, may also require approval by creditors.
