# Draft email: the two directors — where the Ads account stood, and where it is now

Subject: Google Ads — where the account stood, and where it is now

---

Hi both,

A short update on the Google Ads account, a month after I started going through it.

**Where it stood**

Google was being told that a tap on the phone number counted as a new customer, and
was never told when a real enquiry came in. So it went out and bought the cheapest
clicks it could find rather than the work we actually want.

You can see the effect in what we were paying for. Over two weeks in August the
search side of the account took 67 clicks costing £654 and produced one enquiry:

- 43% went on people trying to close or dissolve a company themselves
- 33% went on people searching for rival firms by name, Bright and Co and Solaris
  Law among them, at £62 and £49 a click
- 7% went on liquidation, the work we most want

At the same time the liquidation ad group held 413 keywords and 412 of them were
switched off. The only live one was "closure of a business". Nothing anywhere in
the account bid on "creditors voluntary liquidation", which is about 1,600 searches
a month and the clearest statement of intent a director can type.

Ten of the twelve live adverts sent people to the same page whatever they had
searched for, promising a "liquidation fee quote" that the page had stopped
offering in July. In August that page took 88 clicks costing £739 and produced
nothing.

**Where it is now**

Website enquiries feed straight into the CRM, tested from the form through to the
record. That is the important one: Google can now be pointed at real enquiries
instead of phone taps, so the spend follows the work rather than the cheapest click.

Every lead also records which advert brought it in, so we can follow a paid enquiry
through to an instruction won rather than stopping at a count of form fills.

There are now four dedicated landing pages, one for each type of enquiry:
liquidation, company debt, HMRC arrears and winding-up petitions. Until last week
every advert landed on general guidance written for search engines rather than for
someone about to pick up the phone.

I also built our own read-only access to the Ads data. Everything above came out of
it. Before that we were relying on summaries.

**What I have asked Maria to do**

- Make liquidation the primary area of spend
- Switch the liquidation keywords back on and point them at the new page
- Add "creditors voluntary liquidation"
- Repoint the remaining adverts and replace the copy written for the old page

The measurement is the part that had to come first, and that is done. The rest is
now a question of pointing the money at the right searches. I would expect the
picture to look quite different within a month, and for the first time we will be
able to tell.

Piers

## Notes for Piers, not part of the email

**On the £654.** That figure is the search side only, taken from every clicked
search term between 6 and 19 August. It is deliberately not set against the roughly
£6,000 a month the account spends overall, because that total includes Performance
Max and the two do not reconcile on the face of it. If a director asks why the
numbers look small, that is the answer. I left the £6,000 out rather than invite the
question.

**The percentages are complete, not a sample.** All 56 clicked terms from that
fortnight were categorised, so the shares add up to the whole.

**Nothing here claims a result yet.** The pages have been live since 22 August, so
there is no performance to report. The email promises a different picture within a
month rather than asserting one, which is the honest position and also sets up the
next update.
