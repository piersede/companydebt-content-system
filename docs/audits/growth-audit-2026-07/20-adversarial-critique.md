# Adversarial Critique — Commercial Realism Verdicts on All Growth-Audit Recommendations

Critic pass date: 2026-07-10. Lens: commercial realism in a regulated UK insolvency lead-gen market.
Kill criteria applied: DIY steers / undercutting the licensed-practitioner offer; breaches of the site's disclosure-led trust posture; revenue streams incompatible with a professional-services brand; conversion mechanics that don't fit distressed, anxious directors; double-counted upside.
Inputs: recommendation set (61 items across 8 lenses), analysis files 10–17 (16-monetisation and 15-trust read in full; 12-seo-strategy spot-checked), house rules from repo memory (IP-voice no-DIY-steer, never-fake-authorship gate, live pushes owner-only, statutory-fees source of truth, Bernstein/Opus pipeline economics).

Foundation files 01–06 were not on disk at critique time (confirmed; 16-monetisation.md line 4 records the same gap). Verdicts therefore rest on the analysis files' own verified/inference labelling plus the recommendation texts. Where a claim is "inferred from absence" the verdict says so.

### A2. Direct conflicts between recommendations — one side must lose

1. **Gate vs never-gate.** Content-value says gate the sample-letter pack; defensibility says gate the guide. Monetisation (verified reasoning, 16-monetisation §5) says gating damages rankings, AEO citability and the generosity signal, and that distressed directors are a days-to-weeks audience for whom hard gates are both hostile and low-yield. On this site, generosity IS the moat: the disclosure-led posture ("we are not independent") plus ungated statutory-grade help is what distinguishes CD from every rank-chasing competitor. A director at 11pm with a statutory demand who hits an email wall on a template letter does not convert; he goes back to Google. **Gating loses. Optional "email me this letter/guide as a document" wins.**
2. **Retire vs invest in celebrity pages.** Content-value retires the morgue; intent-journeys wants journey blocks on Bobby Davro (the #2 clicked page). Commercial realism: celebrity-gossip searchers are not directors in distress; conversion from this traffic is ~0; on a YMYL domain that just took a 63–89% traffic haircut, a large low-quality-engagement tail is a liability, not an asset. **Retirement wins** (with the backlink-preserving redirects content-value already specifies). Journey blocks on a doomed page is polishing a write-off.
3. **Position data conflict on 'winding up petition'.** Intent-journeys reports pos 35.6 (GSC avg, 3,913 impressions); seo-strategy reports pos 13 (Ahrefs spot, 2,200/mo KD 3). Both can be true (GSC averages across query variants, devices and weeks; Ahrefs samples one SERP) but the business case sizes differently at 13 vs 35. Reconcile before forecasting clicks from this cluster; the build recommendation survives either way because incumbents are DR 3–22.

### A3. House-rule collisions the recommendations under-weight

1. **Never-fake-authorship gate (trust: split author from reviewer).** The rec calls it "a byline-template change, not a content rewrite". That is exactly the framing that would breach the site's own hard gate: printing "Reviewed by John Newton" on pages Newton has not reviewed is fabricated authorship — the same sin as the fabricated citations already found and fixed in this repo. It only works as an ops commitment (the other IPs actually review their topic areas, on a schedule, with evidence) plus a template change. Effort is therefore materially higher than stated.
2. **Live pushes are owner-only (seo-strategy: ship /data/ live).** The audit can recommend the business go live; it cannot be an execution item for the content system. The blocking item is the known pending human visual QA. Framed correctly: finish QA + trust layer, then Piers pushes.
3. **No promises ops can't keep (cro: emergency conversion tier).** "Priority callback" and out-of-hours language on winding-up-petition pages is a trust breach the moment a director with a 7-day court clock calls at 9pm and gets voicemail. For this audience a broken urgency promise is worse than no promise. Everything in that rec is good ONLY if the phone team signs the SLA first.
4. **FCA perimeter (monetisation: PGI + personal-debt referrals).** Debt counselling for individuals is FCA-regulated; insurance introduction sits inside the insurance-distribution regime unless a specific exemption applies. The rec's own file rates partner risk "Real" and calls the sector reputationally scummy. Thin fees, real trust downside, regulatory homework not yet done — pilot only after written legal/FCA positioning, and after the core funnel work (which carries none of this risk) has shipped.
5. **No-DIY-steer rule.** Checked against the "no money to close", first-Gazette, and TTP recommendations: all pass as framed. The redundancy-funded-CVL angle is the rare case where the honest answer and the commercial answer are the same sentence. The TTP rec explicitly keeps the IP-voice rule. No kills needed on this ground — but any execution of the strike-off/Gazette pages must route "you may be able to object/strike off yourself" intent into "here is when that goes wrong and what it costs you" rather than a how-to.

### A4. The one number that disciplines everything

16-monetisation §2 (verified pricing, GSC-derived): ~22 commercially relevant UK visitors/day; one appointment ≈ £4k–£6k; one extra appointment/month ≈ £48k–£72k/yr. Consequences the recommendation set mostly respects but sometimes forgets:
- Conversion/lead-value work dominates traffic work pound-for-pound at current volume. The highest-conviction items are the form/phone/test fixes, not new pages.
- A/B testing is statistically impossible at this volume (cro correctly notes this); any rec implying test-and-learn conversion optimisation is fantasy. Ship judgement-led changes, measure via call/form attribution.
- Every "£X/mo Ahrefs traffic value" claim is decoration. The only honest denominators are enquiries and appointments.
