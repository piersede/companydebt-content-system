## 1. Executive summary: what the site should become

**The strategic identity: the UK's most transparent insolvency practice online, wrapped in the country's most citable fresh insolvency data.**

Company Debt is not a publisher and should stop behaving like one. Its economics are appointment-shaped: one CVL fee is £4,000 to £6,000 +VAT, one MVL £3,000 to £5,000 +VAT (both published on the live money pages), so a single extra appointment per month is worth roughly £48k to £72k a year, several multiples of the entire Ahrefs-estimated "traffic value" of the site's rankings (~$2,349/mo). UK organic traffic is ~22 clicks a day and fell 89% between March 2025 (5,232 clicks/mo) and June 2026 (576/mo) while average position improved, which is the signature of AI Overviews and a quality reappraisal paying out less per ranking, not a rankings collapse. The definitional information business is gone and will not return. What survived is instructive: urgent, personal, need-a-human distress queries (cant-pay-VAT and cant-pay-PAYE at #2, liquidation-cost at #4) and the new data hub signal ("insolvency statistics" at position ~9.4 with 6.8% CTR, the only new winning query on the site).

The site should therefore become three mutually reinforcing things:

1. **A conversion-grade professional-services storefront** for the ~80 pages that can pay: HMRC arrears, liquidation routes, costs, personal-liability fears, winding-up urgency, and solvent closure. Service-shaped pages, honest triage, instrumented phone and forms, published fees front and centre.
2. **A citable data authority** (the /data/ hub, petition tracker, payment-practices dataset) that earns the deep links and AI citations the money pages lack, because fresh numbers are the one content asset AI answers must cite rather than absorb.
3. **A visibly trustworthy entity**: the Which?-grade internal governance (14-check gate, named-IP sign-off, disclosure rules) published where users, journalists, raters and answer engines can see it. Today the author byline 404s, /editorial-standards/ 404s, and a "Trusted by Thousands" homepage claim sits above "Read our 9 reviews" on the money pages.

**The five biggest moves:** (1) instrument the phone and qualify the form before any other conversion change; (2) fix the liquidation money core in one pass per page (de-cannibalise, reshape CVL/MVL to service intent, rebuild the hub, add the missing internal-anchor layer); (3) ship the /data/ hub live with a citation-grade trust layer and repoint the 192-link footer slot at it; (4) build the two missing funnels (MVL calculator for solvent shoppers; result-first 30-Second Test plus fear-cluster triage for distressed directors); (5) publish the trust layer (author entities, editorial standards, entity map) and prune the dead tail while stopping definitional and newsjack production for good.

## 2. Current state: the honest picture

### 2.1 Business model

Verified on-page: first-party lead generation for licensed insolvency practitioner appointments, two disclosed streams: direct appointments via affiliated AABRS IPs (four named, Chris Andersen IP 16070 fronting all content) and disclosed network referral fees. Published pricing (CVL £4,000 to £6,000 +VAT; MVL £3,000 to £5,000 +VAT with a £17,750 worked tax-saving example) and a sector-unusual non-independence disclosure on money pages. No ads, no affiliate links. Conversion assets: one untracked freephone (0800 074 6757, 4 to 8 instances per page), a 4-field unqualified form, a 30-Second Insolvency Test that gates results behind mandatory contact details, an ungated PDF guide, and zero email capture of any kind.

### 2.3 Structural defects (all verified)

- **Cannibalisation:** three strike-off pages split ~120k impressions with best position 13.8; the hub outranks its own CVL child on CVL terms; three cost pages chase one $10-CPC intent; four competing sector layers with 8 direct duplicates.

### 2.4 Competitive position

DR 42 vs Begbies/RBR at scale, but the binding constraint on the money SERPs is not authority: competitors rank top-5 for CVL/MVL with 2 to 16 referring domains and plain service-entity pages. On Google AI Overviews, the surface actually suppressing clicks, companydebt.com has 0 direct AIO citations and 30 AIO keywords vs RBR's 746 (140 pages) and Begbies' 692, despite ChatGPT parity (17 vs Begbies' 22 citations). Brand demand is ~137 clicks/yr and "company debt" is treated by Google as a generic query (AI Overview plus four ads). The firm cannot out-brand Begbies; it can out-disclose, out-freshen and out-verify them.

## 3. The strategy: one system, not seven page types

The parts must feed each other in a specific direction. Today each layer exists but the plumbing between them does not.

**Money pages** (CVL, MVL, cost, TTP, WUP, strike-off, cant-pay trio) are the terminal conversion assets. They need: service-shaped first viewports (fees, timeline, eligibility, a route to a person), de-cannibalised intent boundaries, an inbound contextual-anchor layer from the traffic pages (from today's 1 anchor to 8+ into CVL), and fresh-data citations from /data/ for E-E-A-T.

**Advice guides and the Q&A long tail** are the acquisition surface. Their job is not clicks for their own sake but routing: the liability/fear constellation (~1,750 clicks/yr: shareholders-liable 904, losing-house 264, PG insurance 179 and rising) gets triage blocks and exposure-conversation CTAs feeding the (rebuilt) test and the money pages. The accelerating /liquidation/ Q&A tail is protected as a do-not-cull moat and extended only on per-question GSC evidence.

**The data hub** is the link and citation engine. Journalists and AI answer layers must cite fresh numbers; the hub earns the deep links the money pages need (money pages currently hold positions 12 to 15 with 0 to 21 RDs) and the AIO citations the domain currently has zero of. It requires the trust layer (methodology, named analyst, press contact, CSV, cite-block) to pass a journalist's 30-second check, reciprocal body links with the money pages (CVL to cvl-statistics, WUP to the petition tracker, and back), and the 192-link footer slot. The outreach conductor throttles until that layer ships.

**The trust layer** (author entities, editorial standards, entity map, honest review counts, date honesty) is what makes all of the above believable to a panicking director, a quality rater and an answer engine simultaneously. It is mostly publishing truth that already exists internally.

**The conversion layer** (instrumented phone, qualified form, result-first test, MVL calculator, expectation-setting copy) turns the routed visitor into an appointment. At ~22 UK clicks/day, A/B testing is statistically impossible; call-level and form-level attribution is the only ground truth, which is why instrumentation is sequenced first.

**The first-party layer** (post-enquiry email sequence, monthly stats briefing, accountant referrer page) is the only demand Google and AI Overviews cannot repossess. It is built once, softly (optional email delivery, never a hard gate; the generosity signal is the moat).

**Editorial capacity** freed by stopping definitional/newsjack production and cutting the 25-page sector rewrite to ~8 demand-backed pages is what funds the gap work above. The Opus/Bernstein pipeline is the site's most expensive production asset; it should touch each money page once (one gated pass carrying the de-cannibalisation, the service-intent reshape and the first-screen intent pattern together), not three times under three project names.

1. **Definitional and newsjack production, permanently.** Misfeasance: 40k impressions at 0.01% CTR. AI Overviews have absorbed this class; new explainers are training data for competitors' citations. Editorial-policy change, effective immediately.
2. **The 25-page sector rewrite as scoped.** No legacy /sectors/ post appears in the top ~120 GSC pages (inference from absence, stated as such); 8 direct duplicates exist.
