# Adversarial Critique — Commercial Realism Lens

Growth audit critic pass. Date: 2026-07-10. Lens: commercial realism in a regulated UK insolvency lead-gen market.
Judged: 61 recommendations across 8 lens files. Verdicts: 35 keep / 25 modify / 1 kill.

Grounding: recommendation evidence as stated (verified vs inference flags respected), 16-monetisation.md read in full for the business-model restatement (two disclosed revenue streams: direct AABRS IP appointments + network referral fees; CVL £4,000–£6,000 +VAT, MVL £3,000–£5,000 +VAT; ~22 UK organic clicks/day; appointment-shaped economics). House rules applied as hard constraints: no DIY steer that undercuts the IP offer; never fake authorship; soft capture only, never hard-gating help content; never promise ops that aren't staffed.

## The four structural findings

### 1. One genuine trust breach: hard-gating the sample letters (KILLED)
[content-value] "Gate the sample-letter pack" is the only recommendation that fails the lens outright. It contradicts the monetisation analysis's own verified finding (16-monetisation.md §5: gating "would damage rankings, AEO citability, and the generosity signal"), and it contradicts two sibling recs ([cro] soft capture "without hard-gating, which would be wrong in this high-anxiety regulated context"; [monetisation] "never gating the letters/PDF"). Commercially: a professional-services brand whose moat is disclosure-led candour does not put an email wall between a panicking director and a template letter to HMRC. The identical capture is achieved by the optional "email me this letter" pattern in [monetisation] "Add post-enquiry email sequence" at zero trust cost. ~450 clicks/yr of distressed traffic is also far too small a base to justify the ranking/AEO downside.

### 3. Promises ops can't keep (modified wherever they appear)
Three recs write cheques the phone room hasn't agreed to cash:
- [cro] Emergency tier on WUP pages: a "priority callback promise" with no out-of-hours staffing is a catastrophic trust failure for a director with a frozen account — and a complaints risk for a regulated-adjacent brand. Keep the hearing-date micro-form and sticky mobile call bar; promise only what Mon–Fri 8–8 can honour ("book the first slot tomorrow, 8am").
- [cro] Expectation-setting copy "a licensed IP (not a salesperson) calls you": verified business model has TWO streams — direct AABRS IPs and network referral — so this copy is plausibly false for a subset of leads. Write copy true for every lead, or it creates the exact breach it's meant to fix.
- [defensibility] Payment-practices + petition-tracker PR cadence: "monthly IP-quoted release notes" is an ops dependency on named humans committing quotes monthly. Secure that commitment before promising journalists a cadence. (Also: the petition tracker is already built on staging — the real rec is ship it, then build payment-practices.)

### 4. Truth constraints on trust fixes (modified)
- [trust] Split author from reviewer: crediting Newton/Bradstock/Meadows as reviewers is only legitimate if they actually review — the site's own never-fake-authorship rule makes name-stamping a hard breach, and IPs lending licence-backed names to unreviewed YMYL content carry personal regulatory exposure. Ops change (real review rota) first, byline template second. Not "a byline-template change".
- [trust] Case studies: insolvency is publicly recorded (Gazette); concrete figures + sector + timeline can re-identify a client. Consent or aggregation sufficient to prevent re-identification, IP sign-off, and never composites-presented-as-real. Fewer verified cases beat eight manufactured ones.
- [defensibility] Entity split "reviews consolidation": must not migrate AABRS reviews onto Company Debt or vice versa — that is review-integrity fraud in miniature.
- [trust] Review collection: solicit compliantly — no incentives, no cherry-picking gate.

## Regulated-market judgments on the contested revenue plays

- **[monetisation] No display ads / generic affiliates — KEEP, adopt as standing policy.** The arithmetic (£250–£500/yr vs one £4k–£6k CVL fee), the mobile-performance cost on the device producing 55% of clicks, and the QRG/YMYL optics all point one way; no ranked competitor runs ads. Strongest "do nothing" call in the audit.
- **[monetisation] PGI + personal-debt referral pilot — MODIFY: split it.** Personal-insolvency referral to a named FCA-authorised partner, disclosed in the existing "we are not independent" language with MoneyHelper signposted, is defensible and genuinely incremental — pilot small. The PGI introducer half should be dropped: (a) product-market mismatch — PGI is bought before distress, CD's PG traffic arrives after guarantees have been signed and the company is wobbling, when insurers won't write cover, so conversion is near-zero; (b) insurance distribution is FCA-regulated activity needing authorisation or careful exemption structuring; (c) selling insurance-adjacent product to panicking directors is the wrong look for a licensed-practitioner brand. The rec's own caveat ("a bad partner costs more trust than the fees earn") is the operative truth — in the IVA sector especially.
- **[seo-strategy] No-money-to-close page / [monetisation] redundancy-funded CVL — KEEP/MODIFY with a compliance guard.** The redundancy-funded model is the honest, on-model answer to "can't afford an IP", but this exact framing is where the Insolvency Service has scrutinised misleading "free liquidation" marketing. Accurate RPS/eligibility language; never read like the "your redundancy pays for it" mills. Fully compatible with the no-DIY-steer rule.
- **[intent-journeys] Solvent-closure decision layer — MODIFY, one guard.** Presenting strike-off honestly where it is right (£25k rule, clean balance sheet) is trust-building, not DIY steer — the house rule protects the insolvency offer, and a solvent £8k-reserves company was never an MVL buyer. Keep the honesty; route the borderline cases to the quote funnel.
- **[intent-journeys] Accountant-door creditor-side flag — right instinct.** Arming creditors on a domain promising directors confidentiality is a real conflict-of-interest optic; it stays an owner decision, and SEO logic must not drag the site into it sideways (the petition tracker will attract creditors anyway once /data/ ships).
- **[content-value]/[seo-strategy] TTP — the highest DIY-steer-risk surface on the site.** Every TTP addition needs the IP-voice check ("never lead with call HMRC yourself"). The cheap on-page version first; a cluster only if the head term responds.
- **[cro] 30-Second Test result-first — KEEP despite the lead-volume risk.** The tool currently breaks an explicit on-page promise ("see your result immediately... no signup required") — leads acquired via a broken promise are the wrong leads for a £5k trust purchase. On-screen verdict wording must stay non-determinative ("appears unable to pay its debts as they fall due — speak to a licensed IP"), not a legal finding.
- **[content-value] Celebrity morgue — MODIFY the kill-list discipline.** Direction right, but don't 410 pages currently earning clicks (Bobby Davro is the #2 clicked page) or holding refdomains until journey blocks have been tried for a quarter on the top earners; deleting 11% of clicks in a -89% year needs owner sign-off. 410 only true zero-click, zero-link pages. This reconciles with [intent-journeys] stop-loss, which is kept.
- **[monetisation] Qualify the form — MODIFY: strip the manufactured number.** The £50k–£200k/yr figure rests on an unverified 10–20% yield-lift assumption over an unmeasured base (no lead-volume data exists — that's what [cro] instrumentation is for). The actions stand on their own; keep qualifiers strictly optional.
