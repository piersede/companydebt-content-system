<p>Much is talked of the demise of UK manufacturing, but this remains a vital and dynamic sector &#8211;&nbsp; this country is the ninth largest manufacturing nation in the world and accounts for 11% of GDP.</p>



<p>It should therefore play a key role as the UK looks to move into recovery after the pandemic. However there remain challenges – these include labour shortages and a majority of firms say they are finding it to attract and retain skilled workers. What is more, production has been affected because staff has been self-isolating.</p>



<p>Brexit continues to cause problems, many firms report supply chain delays and the UK has yet to devise an agreed ‘kitemark’ scheme to show the EU that British goods are safe. Further, so-called lean manufacturing techniques require substantial investment into machinery, processes, and technology.</p>



<p>Despite this, recent output suggests that expectations are being exceeded and there is diversity in the sector &#8211; the UK has both established manufacturing companies and a healthy start-up sector across a wide range of sectors.</p>



<p>The government is also keen to support manufacturing and has recently</p>



<p>announced £53 million of new funding to drive the development of digital manufacturing technologies.</p>
