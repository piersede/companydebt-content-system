## 1. Core Positioning

The page should not assume the visitor has already decided to liquidate the company.

Many PPC visitors will only know that:

- the company cannot pay HMRC, suppliers or other creditors;
- cash flow is getting worse;
- creditors are applying pressure;
- they may need to close the company;
- they want to know whether the business can still be saved.

**Main promise:** a confidential initial assessment that helps a limited-company director understand the realistic options, likely costs and next steps.

Liquidation remains an important possible outcome, but it should not be presented as the visitor's only option before the company's position has been reviewed. This is a strategic repositioning from the live page (liquidation-quote-only) to an options-first assessment.

### FAQs — COMPANY DEBT AND INSOLVENCY

**Do I Need to Know Which Option the Company Requires?**
No. Many directors contact us before they know whether the company can continue, needs restructuring or should close. Tell us what is happening and we will explain which options appear realistic. You are not expected to understand insolvency procedures before asking for help.

**Can the Company Still Be Rescued?**
Possibly. This depends on whether the underlying business is viable, why the financial problems arose and whether the company can meet its future costs. Possible solutions may include improving cash flow, negotiating with creditors, agreeing a Time to Pay arrangement with HMRC or considering a formal restructuring procedure. We will also tell you honestly if rescue does not appear realistic.

**How Much Does It Cost to Liquidate a Company?**
The cost depends on factors including the number of creditors, employees and company assets, the quality of the accounting records and the complexity of the case. The figures you provide can be used to give an initial indication. The final fee and everything included in it should be explained clearly before you decide whether to proceed.

**Do I Have to Proceed After Making an Enquiry?**
No. Completing the form or speaking with our team does not commit you to a formal insolvency procedure. You can ask questions, consider the information and decide what to do without any pressure to appoint us.

**Will the Company's Debts Become My Personal Debts?**
Directors are not normally personally responsible for debts taken out in the company's name. However, you may remain responsible for debts covered by a personal guarantee. Personal liability can also arise in some circumstances involving director conduct or particular liabilities. We will ask about any personal guarantees or related concerns during the consultation.

**Could I Qualify for Director Redundancy?**
Some company directors may qualify for statutory redundancy and other employment-related payments if they were also genuine employees of the company. Eligibility depends on factors including employment status, working arrangements and length of service. The Redundancy Payments Service assesses each claim and makes the final decision.

**How Long Does a Company Liquidation Take?**
The timescale varies according to the company's size and complexity, the assets that need to be realised and any matters that require investigation. A typical liquidation may remain open for 12 to 18 months, although the director's main involvement is usually concentrated near the beginning of the process. The likely timescale should be explained before you proceed.

## 5. Implementation Dependencies

The final wording must match what the form actually does.

**If the form produces an immediate on-screen result** — use wording such as "See My Initial Assessment", "See My Options and Estimated Costs", or "Get an Immediate Initial Assessment". The result must make clear it's indicative and does not replace a review by an insolvency professional.

**If the form only generates a telephone enquiry** — do not promise an immediate assessment, result or quote. Use wording such as "Understand My Options", "Request a Confidential Consultation", or "Speak to an Insolvency Adviser". State when the visitor can expect to be contacted.

## 6. Copy Principles for the Design Stage

- Make it clear immediately that the service is for limited-company directors.
- Do not assume the visitor has chosen liquidation.
- Do not suggest that every company can be rescued.
- Avoid unnecessary insolvency terminology above the form.
- Explain the outcome of submitting the form precisely.
- Keep confidentiality and lack of obligation visible near every major call to action.
- Use real people, named advisers and verifiable credentials wherever possible.
- Retain liquidation wording in supporting copy for PPC relevance, but not as the sole proposition.
