1. THE DROP-OFF. I read the "Which Route Fits Your Company" section fine, but I started
skimming hard once I hit the first big table, because it just restates what the paragraph

rows of legal cross-referencing (wrongful trading, misfeasance, restoration, false

there, picking out headings and bold bits, not reading sentence by sentence. I did come
back and read properly from "Personal exposure" onward because that's the bit I actually
came for.

2. WORDS I DIDN'T KNOW, IN ORDER: "strike-off" (explained a line later, fine). "Members'
Voluntary Liquidation", told what "members" means, fine. "Creditors' Voluntary Liquidation"
(CVL), not defined on first use in the "Six positions" list, only later. "bona vacantia",
defined ("ownerless property"), fine, but I had to wait for it. "wrongful trading (s214 IA
1986)" and "misfeasance (s212 IA 1986)", these just sit in a table with a section number,
no plain description of what either actually means until the row's third column, and even
that is dense. "Personal Liability Notices" / "Joint and Several Liability Notices", never
explained at all, just named. "the Official Receiver", explained ("a government officer"),
fine. "statutory demand", explained inline, fine. "s455 charge" ("Overdrawn DLA at

manual codes quoted like I'm supposed to recognise them. "Business Asset Disposal Relief",
explained enough to use. "anti-phoenix rules", explained reasonably.

The ones that actually stopped me acting: the s212/s214 legal references and the s455
charge. I don't know if those apply to me and the page doesn't tell me how to check. The
rest I could skate past because the page immediately restates them in plain words.

3. THE TWO QUESTIONS I CAME WITH. (a) Do I know what to do first? Yes, and it says it
early: "Take the free insolvency test if there is any doubt" and "Work the checklist above
in order. The formal application is the last step, not the first." That's clear. (b) Do I
know if I personally have to pay and if my house is at risk? Yes, this is answered directly
and I believe it: "Company debt is not, by default, director debt" and "Closing the company
does not, by itself, put your home at risk. Company debts stay with the company. The risk
comes from anything you signed personally: a guarantee, an indemnity, or a charge over your
property." That's the sentence I was looking for. It also tells me what to actually go and
check: "Go through your paperwork for anything you signed personally: guarantees,
indemnities, equipment finance."

4. THE MONEY. Yes, told plainly, with real numbers: "Creditors' Voluntary Liquidation:
3,500 pounds plus VAT as a fixed fee for a straightforward case, plus roughly 500 to 1,500
pounds of disbursements... about 4,000 to 5,000 pounds all in." That's a number I can plan
around. One thing that nagged me: "Where the company cannot fund that, the two usual
answers are a director redundancy claim... or funding from a director or a third party." So
if the company's got nothing left, it might come out of my own pocket after all. The page
doesn't hide this, but it's a long way down the page for something this important to me.
