# Statistics Page Outline

**Page class:** data_reference  
**Use for:** Official data releases, trend series, sector breakdowns — where the primary value is helping a director or adviser read authoritative figures correctly rather than explaining a procedure.

---

## H-tag structure

```
H1: [Topic] Statistics: [What They Show and What They Don't]
    Decision-framed title. Not "An Overview of X Statistics."

H2: [Topic] at a Glance
  H3: Quick Answer: What the Latest Numbers Show
      Two paragraphs max. Lead with the specific current figure and its source date.
      State direction (rising / falling / stable) before you state the level.
  H3: Where the Headline [Number] Is Misleading
      Name the distortion immediately. Don't bury it.
  H3: Main Risk [Directors / Advisers] Should Watch in the Trend Data
      One specific signal — not a list of generic cautions.

H2: What "[Topic] Statistics" Cover (and What They Don't)
  H3: [Category 1 — what is counted]
  H3: [Category 2 — what is counted, and how it differs]
  H3: [The Excluded Channel — what the data omits and why that matters]

H2: How [These Statistics] Are Compiled
  H3: [Primary source agency and methodology]
  H3: [Cross-reference source — ONS, HMRC, Companies House, etc.]
  H3: Lag and Revision in the Numbers
      Mandatory for any government statistics series.

H2: What the Current Numbers Show
  H3: [Primary rate or headline metric] — use the confirmed figure, not a band
  H3: Sector Breakdown (Where the Pressure Is Concentrated)
  H3: [Second data series — personal / regional / sub-procedure]

H2: Where the [Topic] Numbers Are Misleading
  H3: [Distortion 1 — structural, e.g. zombie filings]
  H3: [Distortion 2 — definitional gap, e.g. excluded exit route]
  H3: [Distortion 3 — lag or pipeline effect]

H2: What [Directors / Advisers] Should Do With [These] Statistics
  H3: [Correct comparison method — not year-on-year raw]
  H3: [Cross-reference signal — what to read alongside]
  H3: Use Statistics for [Decision Context], Not the Press Coverage

H2: Related [Topic] Guides
    Internal links to the canonical procedure/entity pages the data points toward.
    List format. 4–6 links. Do not pad.

H2: Frequently Asked Questions About [Topic] Statistics
    FAQ schema block. 6–8 questions. Collapse by default.

[Methodology & Disclosure aside — outside H2 flow]
    Structured as <aside class="methodology-block">.
    Covers: editorial position and its data basis; expert sign-off line;
    data-currency caveat; commercial disclosure; sources list with review dates.
    Never an H2. Never inside the main content columns.
```

---

## Rules for this page class

1. **No placeholder bands.** If the current quarter's figure is confirmed from the source, state it. If it is genuinely unknown, say so explicitly and give the most recent confirmed figure with its date.

2. **Source every rate.** Never write "the insolvency rate" — write "the Insolvency Service 12-month rolling rate for England and Wales (April 2026)." Precision is editorial honesty, not pedantry.

3. **Name distortions before trends.** If the headline overstates or understates, say so in the at-a-glance section before the reader has committed to a reading.

4. **Route to procedure pages.** Every sector trend should link out to the relevant entity owner or process guide page on the site. Data reference pages don't compete with procedure pages; they feed them.

5. **Methodology aside is non-negotiable.** Data pages carry higher disclosure obligations than opinion or advice pages. The aside must include: what reading the data supports, how it was checked, who signed off procedural detail, a data-currency caveat, and commercial disclosure.

6. **Update cadence note.** State clearly in the at-a-glance section how often the source publishes and when the next release is expected. This page class goes stale faster than any other.
