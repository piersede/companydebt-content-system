# Process Guide Overlay

- focus on execution-stage usefulness
- make step order, documents, and stakeholder impacts explicit
- include failure points and urgent detours
- do not drift back into broad owner-page definition

## Open by locating the reader in the process

Readers arrive at different points and need different dates. Branch early,
before the detail, and let them self-select:

- nothing has started
- the decision is made but not yet formalised
- the formal step has happened
- a third party has forced the timetable
- the process is already under way

This is more useful to someone under pressure than opening with definitions,
and it is the fastest way to stop a page reading as though it is addressed to
somebody further along than the reader is.

## Split the reader's obligations from the practitioner's

Say which steps the reader must personally perform and which belong to the
appointed office-holder. Give them separate sections and label them plainly.

Listing both in one sequence implies the reader owes all of them. On a
deadlines page that is actively harmful: it manufactures obligations the
reader does not have, and buries the ones they do.

## Answer "what if I have already missed it"

A process page attracts readers who believe they are already late. Telling
them repeatedly how serious the steps are, without ever addressing the miss,
leaves the page's most anxious question unanswered.

Include a short section covering:

- the consequence depends on which step was missed, with the range named
- do not conceal it, and do not reconstruct documents to improve the timing
- identify the specific obligation and when it fell, then take advice

This turns a page that describes a process into one that helps somebody who is
inside it and frightened.
