# Legal Compliance Overlay

- anchor key claims to statute, case law, or formal guidance
- explain trigger conditions and good-practice behaviors
- do not speculate or sensationalize
- end with practical protective action and escalation boundaries

## State the jurisdiction before anything else

Say which jurisdiction the page describes, in plain words, near the opening.
Never let the reader infer it from the legislation you happen to cite.

"The deadlines below apply to company liquidations in England and Wales.
Scotland and Northern Ireland run to different procedural rules."

One sentence materially improves the legal safety of a whole page. A page that
cites the Insolvency (England and Wales) Rules 2016 and the London Gazette but
never names the jurisdiction is unsafe for a Scottish or Northern Irish reader.

## Authority comes from precision, not confidence

This is the governing rule of the overlay. Be extremely definite where the law
is definite, and deliberately careful where it is not.

Before writing any categorical sentence, classify it as one of three things:

1. **This is exactly what the law says.** Cite it and be firm.
2. **This is what usually happens in the cases we handle.** Mark it as our
   experience, and only if a practitioner would stand behind it.
3. **This is simply the clearest way I have found to describe it.** Rewrite it
   until it is 1 or 2, or cut it.

Those are three different things. Signal the difference in the sentence itself.

**The failure this prevents:** a sentence becomes inaccurate because the writer
wanted it to land harder. Every legal error found on the liquidation-deadlines
page in the 2026-08-18 practitioner review had that shape. Good copy reaching
for certainty is where mistakes enter legal content, not sloppiness.

## Stop on the categorical words

Treat each of these as a checkpoint requiring the three-way test above:

`every` · `always` · `never` · `all` · `permanently` · `automatically` ·
`void` · `immediately` · `only` · `cannot` · `the same`

Worked corrections from live pages:

- "that date drives **every** deadline on this page" -> it drove the early CVL
  deadlines only; the same page listed six deadlines running from other events.
- "dissolution removes the company from the register **permanently**" ->
  restoration is possible in limited circumstances.
- "a late claim and no claim produce **the same** result" -> an office-holder
  has discretion over a late proof in some circumstances.

## Give the size of a risk, not only its existence

Naming a consequence and withholding its scale is not caution, it is the gap
where a worried reader's imagination does the work. It reads as fear-mongering
even when every word is true.

State the exposure, then its likelihood or its limits, in the same passage:

- personal liability -> who has to prove what, and whether the outcome is routine
- a criminal offence -> and the constructive step that avoids it
- a conduct report -> and that most lead to nothing further

This is also the honest way to be reassuring. Proportion beats softer adjectives.

## Separate the reader's obligations from the office-holder's

On any procedure page, make explicit which deadlines the reader must personally
meet and which belong to the practitioner once appointed. Use separate sections
and say so plainly.

A distressed director should never have to carry the practitioner's diary as
well as their own. This reduces cognitive load more than any tone change.

## Known accuracy traps, UK insolvency

Verified corrections from practitioner review. Check these by name before
publishing anything that touches them.

| Trap | The correct position |
|---|---|
| Statement of affairs described as "sworn" | It is verified by a **statement of truth**. No oath, deponent or commissioner. "Sworn" implies legal machinery that is not required. |
| Notice of liquidator's appointment | **14 days** under s109, not 15. s109 also requires the liquidator to advertise the appointment in the Gazette. |
| Companies House appointment form | **600CH** for England and Wales appointments from 9 December 2017. The old form 600 applies only before that date. |
| s127 void dispositions | Run from **presentation of the petition** (s129 deems the winding up to commence then), not from the winding-up order. This is also why banks react to a petition. |
| Petition service period | There is **no fixed 21-day** service period. The 21 days directors have in mind belongs to a **statutory demand**. |
| Dissolution | Ends normal legal existence; **restoration is possible** in limited circumstances. |
| Wrongful trading (s214) | Test is no reasonable prospect of avoiding insolvent liquidation **or insolvent administration**, then whether every step was taken with a view to minimising potential loss to creditors. Taking advice is **not itself a defence**. |
| Conduct reports | Required in every insolvent liquidation. Their existence does **not** imply wrongdoing or that an investigation follows. |

## Do not name an audience you do not serve

If the title promises directors and creditors, the page owes creditors a real
section, not one paragraph. Otherwise narrow the title. Widening a title to
catch more search terms, without the content behind it, fails the reader who
arrives on the strength of it.
